# Chest X-Ray Interpretation — Educational Guide

Title: Chest X-Ray Interpretation Guide
Document Type: educational

## Systematic Approach

A systematic approach to chest X-ray (CXR) interpretation ensures no pathology is missed. Use the ABCDE approach:

### A — Airway
- Trachea: Midline or deviated?
- Tracheal deviation suggests tension pneumothorax, large pleural effusion, or mediastinal mass
- Carina: Normal bifurcation angle

### B — Bones
- Rib fractures
- Lytic or blastic lesions
- Cervical spine alignment
- Shoulder joints

### C — Cardiac
- Heart size: Cardiothoracic ratio (CTR) < 0.5 on PA film is normal
- Cardiomegaly: CTR > 0.5
- Heart borders: Right (right atrium), Left (left ventricle)
- Aortic knob
- Pericardial effusion (water bottle sign)

### D — Diaphragm
- Right hemidiaphragm normally higher than left
- Flattened diaphragm: Hyperinflation (COPD, asthma)
- Elevated hemidiaphragm: Phrenic nerve palsy, hepatomegaly, subphrenic abscess
- Free air under diaphragm: Pneumoperitoneum (perforation)

### E — Everything else (Lung fields, soft tissue, lines/tubes)

## Common Lung Pathologies on CXR

### Consolidation
- Opacification of lung parenchyma with air bronchograms
- Causes: Pneumonia (most common), pulmonary hemorrhage, organizing pneumonia
- Lobar pattern: Typical bacterial pneumonia
- Patchy/bilateral: Atypical pneumonia, aspiration

### Pleural Effusion
- Blunting of costophrenic angle (> 200 mL needed on PA film)
- Meniscus sign
- Massive: Opacification of hemithorax with mediastinal shift away
- Causes: Heart failure, infection (parapneumonic/empyema), malignancy, PE

### Pneumothorax
- Visible visceral pleural line
- Absence of lung markings beyond the pleural line
- Tension pneumothorax: Mediastinal shift away, flattened diaphragm

### Interstitial Patterns
- Reticular: Pulmonary fibrosis, interstitial lung disease
- Nodular: Miliary TB, metastases, sarcoidosis
- Ground glass: Viral pneumonia, PCP, early ARDS

### Mass Lesions
- Solitary pulmonary nodule: < 3 cm
- Lung mass: > 3 cm (higher malignancy concern)
- Evaluate: Shape, margins, calcification, cavitation

### Pulmonary Edema
- Cardiogenic: Bilateral, perihilar "bat wing" distribution, cephalization of vessels, Kerley B lines, pleural effusions
- Non-cardiogenic (ARDS): Bilateral, peripheral, without cephalization

## Limitations
- CXR has limited sensitivity for small nodules, early interstitial disease, and PE
- CT is superior for detailed evaluation
- Clinical correlation is always essential
