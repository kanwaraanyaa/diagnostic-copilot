# Renal Function Assessment — Clinical Overview

Title: Renal Function Assessment
Document Type: educational

## Creatinine

### Overview
Serum creatinine is a waste product of muscle metabolism filtered by the kidneys. It is one of the primary markers used to assess kidney function.

### Normal Values
- Men: 0.7–1.3 mg/dL (62–115 μmol/L)
- Women: 0.6–1.1 mg/dL (53–97 μmol/L)

### Elevated Creatinine

Causes of elevated serum creatinine:
- Acute kidney injury (AKI)
- Chronic kidney disease (CKD)
- Dehydration
- Medications (ACE inhibitors, NSAIDs, aminoglycosides)
- Obstruction of urinary tract
- Rhabdomyolysis
- High protein diet or muscle mass (mild elevation)
- Heart failure (reduced renal perfusion)

### Decreased Creatinine
- Low muscle mass
- Malnutrition
- Liver disease (decreased creatine production)

## Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN)

### Normal Values
- 7–20 mg/dL

### Elevated BUN
Causes:
- Dehydration (prerenal azotemia) — BUN:Creatinine ratio > 20:1
- Kidney disease
- Heart failure
- GI bleeding
- High protein diet
- Catabolic states

## Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR)

### Overview
eGFR is calculated from serum creatinine, age, sex, and race. It is the best overall indicator of kidney function.

### Staging of CKD by eGFR
- Stage 1: eGFR ≥ 90 (normal kidney function with other evidence of damage)
- Stage 2: eGFR 60–89 (mildly decreased)
- Stage 3a: eGFR 45–59 (mild to moderate decrease)
- Stage 3b: eGFR 30–44 (moderate to severe decrease)
- Stage 4: eGFR 15–29 (severely decreased)
- Stage 5: eGFR < 15 (kidney failure)

## Urinalysis

Key findings:
- Proteinuria: May indicate glomerular disease
- Hematuria: Infection, stones, malignancy, glomerulonephritis
- Casts: Red cell casts (glomerulonephritis), white cell casts (pyelonephritis), muddy brown casts (ATN)
- Specific gravity: Hydration status and concentrating ability

## Clinical Context

Renal function assessment should consider:
- Baseline kidney function
- Acute vs. chronic changes
- Medications affecting kidneys
- Volume status
- Comorbidities (diabetes, hypertension)
- Age-related decline in eGFR is common
