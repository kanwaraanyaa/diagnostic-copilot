# Complete Blood Count (CBC) — Interpretation Guide

Title: CBC Interpretation Guide
Document Type: educational

## Overview

The complete blood count (CBC) is one of the most commonly ordered laboratory tests. It provides valuable information about the three major cell lines in blood: red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets.

## White Blood Cell Count (WBC)

Normal range: 4,500–11,000 cells/μL

### Leukocytosis (WBC > 11,000/μL)

Common causes:
- Bacterial infection (most common)
- Inflammatory conditions
- Stress response (physical or emotional)
- Medications (corticosteroids)
- Leukemia and myeloproliferative disorders
- Tissue necrosis
- Smoking

### Leukopenia (WBC < 4,500/μL)

Common causes:
- Viral infections
- Bone marrow suppression
- Autoimmune conditions
- Medications (chemotherapy, certain antibiotics)
- Severe sepsis (late stage)
- Nutritional deficiencies

## Hemoglobin / Hematocrit

Normal hemoglobin: Men 13.5–17.5 g/dL, Women 12.0–16.0 g/dL

### Anemia (Low Hemoglobin)

Classification by MCV:
- Microcytic (MCV < 80 fL): Iron deficiency, thalassemia, chronic disease
- Normocytic (MCV 80–100 fL): Acute blood loss, chronic kidney disease, anemia of chronic disease
- Macrocytic (MCV > 100 fL): B12 deficiency, folate deficiency, liver disease, hypothyroidism

### Polycythemia (High Hemoglobin)

Causes:
- Polycythemia vera
- Chronic hypoxia (COPD, high altitude)
- Dehydration (relative polycythemia)

## Platelet Count

Normal range: 150,000–400,000/μL

### Thrombocytopenia (Platelets < 150,000/μL)

Common causes:
- Immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP)
- Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)
- Liver disease (hypersplenism)
- Medication-induced (heparin — HIT)
- Bone marrow disorders
- Severe infections

### Thrombocytosis (Platelets > 400,000/μL)

Common causes:
- Reactive (infection, inflammation, iron deficiency)
- Essential thrombocythemia
- Post-splenectomy

## Clinical Significance

The CBC should always be interpreted in the clinical context. Isolated laboratory values may be misleading without considering:
- Patient symptoms and history
- Medications
- Acute vs. chronic changes
- Trends over time
- Other laboratory results
