# Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP) — Clinical Overview

Title: Community-Acquired Pneumonia Clinical Guide
Document Type: clinical_guideline

## Definition

Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) is an acute infection of the pulmonary parenchyma acquired outside the hospital setting. It remains one of the most common infectious diseases and a significant cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide.

## Etiology

The most common pathogens include:
- Streptococcus pneumoniae (most frequent bacterial cause)
- Haemophilus influenzae
- Mycoplasma pneumoniae (common in younger adults)
- Chlamydophila pneumoniae
- Respiratory viruses (influenza, SARS-CoV-2, RSV)
- Legionella pneumophila (consider in severe cases)

## Clinical Presentation

Typical symptoms include:
- Cough (productive or non-productive)
- Fever or hypothermia
- Dyspnea (shortness of breath)
- Pleuritic chest pain
- Fatigue and malaise

Physical examination findings may include:
- Tachypnea (respiratory rate > 20/min)
- Tachycardia
- Decreased breath sounds
- Crackles or rales on auscultation
- Dullness to percussion over affected area
- Bronchial breathing

## Laboratory Findings

Common laboratory abnormalities:
- Elevated white blood cell count (WBC > 11,000/μL) — leukocytosis
- Elevated C-reactive protein (CRP)
- Elevated procalcitonin (supports bacterial etiology)
- Possible hypoxemia on arterial blood gas
- Elevated lactate in severe cases

## Imaging

Chest radiograph typically shows:
- Lobar consolidation (typical pneumonia)
- Interstitial or patchy infiltrates (atypical pneumonia)
- Air bronchograms
- Possible pleural effusion

## Differential Diagnosis

Conditions that may present similarly:
- Acute bronchitis
- Pulmonary embolism
- Congestive heart failure
- Lung malignancy
- Tuberculosis
- Hypersensitivity pneumonitis

## Severity Assessment

The CURB-65 score is commonly used:
- Confusion
- Urea > 7 mmol/L
- Respiratory rate ≥ 30/min
- Blood pressure < 90/60 mmHg
- Age ≥ 65 years

Each factor scores 1 point. Score 0-1: outpatient, 2: consider admission, 3-5: consider ICU.

## Complications

- Parapneumonic effusion
- Empyema
- Lung abscess
- Sepsis
- Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS)
