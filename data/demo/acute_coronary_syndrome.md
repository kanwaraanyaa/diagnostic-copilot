# Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) — Clinical Overview

Title: Acute Coronary Syndrome Clinical Guide
Document Type: clinical_guideline

## Definition

Acute coronary syndrome (ACS) encompasses a range of conditions associated with sudden reduction or blockage of blood flow to the heart. It includes:
- ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI)
- Non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI)
- Unstable angina

## Risk Factors

Major risk factors for ACS include:
- Age (men ≥ 45, women ≥ 55)
- Hypertension
- Diabetes mellitus
- Dyslipidemia (elevated LDL, low HDL)
- Smoking
- Family history of premature coronary artery disease
- Obesity
- Sedentary lifestyle

## Clinical Presentation

Typical symptoms:
- Chest pain or pressure (substernal, may radiate to left arm, jaw, or back)
- Diaphoresis (sweating)
- Nausea or vomiting
- Dyspnea
- Palpitations
- Feeling of impending doom

Atypical presentations (more common in women, elderly, diabetic patients):
- Epigastric pain
- Fatigue
- Isolated dyspnea
- Syncope

## Laboratory Findings

Key biomarkers:
- Troponin I or T: Elevated above 99th percentile indicates myocardial injury. Serial measurements recommended. Troponin is the most sensitive and specific biomarker.
- CK-MB: Less specific than troponin; rises 4-6 hours after onset.
- BNP/NT-proBNP: May be elevated indicating heart failure.

Other findings:
- Possible elevated glucose (stress hyperglycemia)
- Elevated WBC (inflammatory response)
- Elevated CRP
- Possible renal function changes (creatinine)

## Electrocardiographic Findings

- ST elevation: STEMI
- ST depression: NSTEMI or unstable angina
- T-wave inversions
- New left bundle branch block
- Q waves (established infarction)

## Imaging

- Chest X-ray: May show cardiomegaly, pulmonary edema
- Echocardiography: Wall motion abnormalities, ejection fraction assessment
- Coronary angiography: Definitive for identifying coronary occlusion

## Differential Diagnosis

Conditions that may present similarly to ACS:
- Pulmonary embolism
- Aortic dissection
- Pericarditis
- Myocarditis
- Pneumothorax
- Musculoskeletal chest pain
- Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)
- Panic disorder

## Complications

- Heart failure
- Cardiogenic shock
- Cardiac arrhythmias
- Mitral regurgitation
- Ventricular septal rupture
- Pericarditis
