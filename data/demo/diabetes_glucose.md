# Glucose Metabolism and Diabetes — Clinical Overview

Title: Glucose Metabolism and Diabetes Overview
Document Type: educational

## Blood Glucose

### Fasting Blood Glucose
- Normal: < 100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L)
- Pre-diabetes: 100–125 mg/dL (5.6–6.9 mmol/L)
- Diabetes: ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L)

### Random Blood Glucose
- Diabetes: ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) with symptoms

### HbA1c
- Normal: < 5.7%
- Pre-diabetes: 5.7–6.4%
- Diabetes: ≥ 6.5%

## Hyperglycemia

### Causes
- Diabetes mellitus (type 1 or type 2)
- Stress hyperglycemia (acute illness, surgery, trauma)
- Medications (corticosteroids, thiazide diuretics)
- Cushing syndrome
- Acromegaly
- Pancreatitis
- Parenteral nutrition

### Clinical Significance
Stress hyperglycemia in hospitalized patients is common and associated with worse outcomes, even without a prior diagnosis of diabetes. It may occur during:
- Acute infections (pneumonia, sepsis)
- Myocardial infarction
- Stroke
- Trauma

## Hypoglycemia

### Definition
Blood glucose < 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L)

### Causes
- Insulin or sulfonylurea use
- Missed meals
- Excessive alcohol
- Adrenal insufficiency
- Hepatic failure
- Insulinoma
- Sepsis

### Symptoms
- Autonomic: Sweating, tremor, palpitations, hunger
- Neuroglycopenic: Confusion, drowsiness, seizures, coma

## Diabetic Emergencies

### Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)
- More common in type 1 diabetes
- Characterized by hyperglycemia, ketosis, metabolic acidosis
- Presents with nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, Kussmaul breathing
- Lab findings: Glucose > 250, pH < 7.3, bicarbonate < 18, positive ketones

### Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS)
- More common in type 2 diabetes
- Severe hyperglycemia (> 600 mg/dL) without significant ketosis
- Profound dehydration
- Altered mental status

## Complications of Chronic Hyperglycemia
- Retinopathy
- Nephropathy
- Neuropathy (peripheral and autonomic)
- Cardiovascular disease
- Peripheral arterial disease
- Increased infection risk
